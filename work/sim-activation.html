01Situation

Activation was buried in a monolith.

SIM activation was buried in a tightly coupled monolith. Deploys were risky and test coverage was near zero. Activation and logistics weren't connected, so purchase, activation and delivery felt like three separate experiences to the customer.

02Constraint

One spike slowed everything.

A traffic spike degraded the whole platform. Activation had to scale, fail and ship without the rest.

03Ownership

Migration lead.

I led the move from the monolith to Go and Node.js services, and wired in the SIM controllers and logistics providers.

04Decision

Run activation and fulfillment as separate services, so one can fail without the other.

THE BOUNDARY Activated is not delivered. Activation and delivery need separate records. A slow logistics dependency should remain visible without rewriting an activation result.

05Model

One purchase. Two services.

Switch the logistics provider off: activation still finishes, and fulfillment waits with a reason until the provider returns.

07Tradeoff

Two services, two states to reconcile.

Separating services creates a need to correlate events and reconcile states across their boundaries. When activation and fulfillment disagreed, reconciliation caught the mismatch and resolved it.
